# BRAND.md - Chief Jumper

## Positioning line (in Dana's language)

**"Climb the org chart. Get laid off anyway."**

Secondary line for meta descriptions and the menu: "A vertical platformer about the corporate ladder. Every run ends in a restructuring."

The name stays **Chief Jumper**. The core concept stays: canvas vertical platformer, dual boosters, job titles as levels, LAID OFF as the death state.

## Palette direction

Corporate-noir pixel: a dark "after-hours office" base with 2 loud accents that carry the satire.

- **Base:** near-black navy (current #0a0a1a family). Keep it. It reads as both night sky and a dead office at 9pm.
- **Pink-slip red (#ff3333 family):** reserved exclusively for termination. LAID OFF header, damage flashes, HR-flavored enemies. Red must always mean "the company is hurting you."
- **Bonus gold (#ffcc44 family):** reserved exclusively for status. Job titles, promotions, NEW BEST, score. Gold must always mean "the ladder."
- **Corporate blue (#3399ff family):** the neutral UI accent, buttons, share card border, player visor. The color of every SaaS dashboard she stares at all day; that is the joke.
- Supporting grays (#888888, #555566) for fine print and disclaimer-toned microcopy.

Rule: never let red and gold share a role. The whole death-card punchline is gold title crushed by red verdict.

## Type system

- **Single family: monospace pixel-adjacent stack** (current monospace is correct; if a webfont is ever added, something like a chunky pixel mono, self-hosted, no CDN). Monospace reads as both retro-game and terminal/HR-system printout, which is the brand.
- Scale, mobile-first canvas: display 28 to 32px bold (CHIEF JUMPER, LAID OFF), heading 14 to 16px bold (job titles, PROMOTED!), body 12 to 14px, fine print 10 to 11px.
- ALL CAPS for anything the "company" says (LAID OFF, PROMOTED!, TAP TO START). Sentence case for anything said to the player as a human ("Your best: 4,120 as Manager").

## Spacing and motion personality

- **Spacing:** generous and poster-like on end screens. The death card is a composition, not a stat dump: big margins, 3 clear tiers (verdict, title, score), fine print at the bottom like a severance letter footer.
- **Motion:** snappy arcade physics in play (nothing floaty; booster feel is the core loop), but **abrupt, bureaucratic transitions** for corporate moments. LAID OFF should slam in with a 1-frame cut or hard stamp effect, not a gentle fade. Promotions get a short gold flourish (under 400ms). Menus pulse subtly. Nothing eases longer than 400ms; she has Celeste-calibrated expectations for responsiveness.
- Screen shake on death: brief, single impact. The layoff is sudden. That is the point.

## Voice and tone rules

1. **Deadpan HR, never winking.** Copy is written by the fictional company, not by a comedian. "Relocating to next floor..." and "You have been impacted" beat any joke that says "lol corporate am I right."
2. **Real corporate vocabulary only:** restructuring, impacted, synergies, per my last email, alignment, headcount. No invented wacky words.
3. **The player is never mocked.** The company is the villain; the player's title and score are always rendered with genuine gold-plated respect. That contrast is the emotional engine.
4. **Short.** Every string fits on a phone canvas. If a line needs a 2nd sentence, cut it.
5. Numbers as digits everywhere (score 4,120, floor 7).

## 3 reference products to measure taste against

1. **Celeste** - input feel, death handling, pixel-art polish bar
2. **Universal Paperclips** - total commitment to a dry corporate bit
3. **Wordle** - the 1-tap, zero-explanation share artifact

## 3 anti-references (never look like this)

1. **Generic AI-template slop:** gradient-purple hero, rounded-2xl cards, emoji-riddled copy, "Built with love" footers. This is a canvas game; chrome outside the canvas should be nearly invisible.
2. **Flash-portal endless jumpers (Doodle Jump clones with ad frames):** cluttered HUDs, coin shops, "RATE US" popups, mascot cuteness. Chief Jumper is dry, not cute.
3. **LinkedIn-motivational aesthetic:** any sincere use of hustle language, lightbulb icons, stock-photo optimism, or blue-gradient corporate uplift. The brand satirizes that world; it must never accidentally resemble it.

## Residual debt to burn

The codebase still carries space-explorer DNA (starfield menu background, Spartan-flavored player sprite naming). Acceptable read: "night sky above the office towers." But no new copy or art may reference planets, space, or sci-fi. The satire owns the theme now.
